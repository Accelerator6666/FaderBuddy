FaderBuddy QC
How every board is tested

Every FaderBuddy board sold by Bezek Labs LLC is tested on a real 60 mm motor fader before it ships.

The board drops into the fixture and a clamp presses it down onto pogo pins. Those pins carry power, the fader’s motor and position wiring, and the I2C lines. From there the fixture programs the board, then talks to it the same way your own controller will.

As the board is tested, the ESP32 host records and validates its behavior, which ultimately gets archived as a test report; see a (real) test report here.

What gets tested

  • Current draw and supply voltage on both power rails
  • The power LED and the status LED
  • Programming the chip, and updating its firmware over I2C
  • Motor movement, position feedback, and self-calibration
  • Touch sensing on the fader cap

What’s in the fixture

  • A real motor fader. A Soundwell 60 mm unit, the same part the board is made for. Nothing is simulated.
  • Pogo pins. One set connects the board to the fader. Another set connects its I2C pins to the test host.
  • An ESP32. This is the host for the test. It sends commands to the board, reads back what happened, and decides pass or fail.
  • A servo and a spring metal contact. The servo swings the contact onto the fader cap and off again. That is how the touch sensing gets tested, in place of a finger.
  • An INA3221 power meter. Watches current and voltage on the 3.3 V and 5 V supplies throughout the test.
  • Polyfuses on both supplies. A board with a short circuit trips them instead of damaging the fixture.
  • An Adafruit UPDI Friend. Loads the first firmware onto a blank chip.
  • An Adafruit CH334F USB hub. Puts the test host and the programmer on one cable to the computer.
  • A thermal printer. Prints the label for each board that passes, using a protocol reverse-engineered from the vendor’s browser extension.
